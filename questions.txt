1. What is the primary objective of a Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) framework in an enterprise environment?
Answer: To align IT operations with business goals while managing risk and ensuring adherence to regulatory requirements.

2. How does AI governance differ from traditional IT governance?
Answer: AI governance specifically addresses algorithmic bias, model transparency, data provenance, and continuous behavioral monitoring of machine learning systems.

3. What is the significance of the NIST AI Risk Management Framework (AI RMF)?
Answer: It provides a structured voluntary guidance model focused on trustworthiness across four core functions: Govern, Map, Measure, and Manage.

4. What does "Risk Appetite" mean in the context of organizational cybersecurity policy?
Answer: The amount and type of risk an organization is willing to pursue or retain before implementing additional controls.

5. What is the purpose of conducting a Data Protection Impact Assessment (DPIA)?
Answer: To identify and minimize data protection risks prior to processing personal or sensitive information, particularly under regulations like GDPR.

6. Define the Principle of Least Privilege (PoLP) and its role in governance.
Answer: Ensuring users and systems only have the absolute minimum access levels necessary to perform their required functions, reducing the blast radius of potential breaches.

7. What is model drift in AI systems, and why is it a governance concern?
Answer: Model drift occurs when an AI model's accuracy degrades over time due to changes in the operating environment or underlying data distributions, impacting reliability and compliance.

8. What is the difference between a security policy, a standard, and a procedure?
Answer: Policies are high-level management directives; standards define mandatory rules or technologies; procedures are step-by-step instructions for execution.

9. How do third-party risk management (TPRM) programs protect an organization?
Answer: By assessing, monitoring, and mitigating security and compliance risks introduced by external vendors and supply chain partners.

10. What is an Incident Response Plan (IRP), and why is executive sign-off critical?
Answer: A structured framework for detecting, responding to, and recovering from cybersecurity incidents; executive sign-off ensures legal, financial, and operational alignment during a crisis.
